Abstract

Detecting maneuvers of non-cooperative satellites is essential for space situational awareness. The interacting multiple model (IMM) filter has been applied to detect unannounced maneuvers, but the standard IMM assumes a fixed Markov transition probability matrix (TPM) that must be tuned without knowledge of the maneuver rate of the target. To avoid the tuning, adaptive-TPM approaches update the TPM online from statistics computed inside the IMM. However, the statistics already depend on the TPM through the interaction step, and the dependence forms a feedback loop. The loop itself can raise the maneuver probability in the absence of a maneuver or suppress its rise when a maneuver occurs. In this paper, the TPM is driven by the normalized innovation squared (NIS) of a reference coast filter that is decoupled from the IMM, and the NIS is mapped continuously to the coast-to-maneuver transition probability. The reference-driven IMM (RD-IMM) is evaluated with four space-based optical sensors tracking a geostationary target. In the simulation, the reference filter keeps its sensitivity after small maneuvers, whereas the NIS of the coast sub-filter of the IMM is reduced by the interaction step. RD-IMM detects 1–3 m/s maneuvers, which the fixed-TPM IMM rarely detects, with a false-declaration rate below the false-alarm probability of 0.05 set for the reference filter. In the first hour after the 1–3 m/s maneuvers, RD-IMM also has a lower position error than the compared filters.

Method

  • Surprisal. The NIS of the reference filter is converted to the χ2 surprisal sk = −log10[1 − F(Ψrefk)], which has one scale for any number of observing sensors.
  • Continuous map. A fading maximum αk = max(ραk−1, sk/c) is mapped by a logistic function to the coast-to-maneuver probability pCM,k between plo = 0.005 and phi = 0.60.
  • Design rule. The constant c = log10(N/PFA) = 3.87 follows from the false-alarm probability PFA = 0.05 over the N = 371 measurement times after warm-up, without training.
  • Internal-NIS drive. The same map driven by the NIS of the coast sub-filter of the IMM closes the loop through the interaction step. The variant is included in the demo to show the reduced surprisal after small maneuvers.

Scenario

  • SensorsFour space-based optical sensors on a 622 km, 96° orbit measure azimuth and elevation every 60 s; two to four observe the target at a time.
  • Truth vs. filtersThe truth includes J2–J6, lunisolar third-body and solar radiation pressure accelerations; the filters propagate J2 only.
  • ManeuverA single impulse of 0.5–10 m/s, in-track or out-of-plane, at tb = 120 min of a 400 min arc; the first 30 min are warm-up.
  • Runs50 Monte Carlo runs per case with the same measurements for all filters; 500 maneuver-free runs for the false-declaration rate.
