RD-IMM: Reference-Driven IMM for Satellite Maneuver Detection

E. Han, S. Choi, S.-H. Kong, Reference-Filter-Driven Transition Probabilities for IMM-Based Satellite Maneuver DetectionManuscript in preparation

An interacting multiple model (IMM) filter tracks a satellite with a coast model and a maneuver model, and switches between them through a transition probability matrix (TPM). Adaptive-TPM methods update the TPM from statistics computed inside the IMM, but those statistics already depend on the TPM through the mixing step. RD-IMM drives the coast-to-maneuver probability pCM from the normalized innovation squared (NIS) of a reference coast filter that is never mixed with the IMM.

Where the drive comes from

Reference filter (RD-IMM) The reference CKF receives the same measurements but neither Πk nor the mode probabilities enter its recursion, so its NIS does not depend on the TPM. After a burn it diverges and its surprisal rises; its covariance is reset when Ψref > 30, and the surprisal returns to its coast level.
Coast sub-filter inside the IMM (internal-NIS drive) Same map, but the NIS is taken from the IMM's coast sub-filter. The mixing step pulls that filter toward the maneuver estimate, which shrinks its innovation after a burn (desensitization). Driving the TPM from the mode probability instead can hold PM above 0.5 without a maneuver (stuck-high, AIMM below).

Choosing the mapping constant c

αk = max(ρ αk−1, sk/c),  ρ = 0.7
pCM,k = plo + (phi − plo) / (1 + e−γ(αk−1))
c = log₁₀(N / PFA) = log₁₀(371 / 0.05) = 3.9 → 4

During coast, Pr{sk ≥ c} = 10−c, so by the union bound c sets how often αk reaches 1 over an arc of N measurement times without a maneuver.
